Frequently Asked Questions

Here are some of the most common questions regarding whole body scans:

  1. What is the cost of a whole body scan? The price of a whole body scan can vary greatly depending on the type of scan, the provider, and your location. It's best to contact the provider directly for specific pricing.
  2. Are whole body scans covered by insurance? Insurance coverage for whole body scans can vary. Some insurance plans may cover certain types of scans if there is a medical reason, and others may not. It's recommended to check with your insurance provider.
  3. What are the risks associated with whole body scans? One potential risk is exposure to radiation. MRI scans do not involve radiation, but CT scans do. Another risk is the possible discovery of incidental findings that may require further investigation and potentially lead to procedures.
  4. How long does a whole body scan take? The duration of a whole body scan can vary based on the type of scan, but they generally last from 30 minutes to a couple of hours.
  5. What can a whole body scan detect? A whole body scan can detect a wide range of potential health concerns, including tumors, aneurysms, and other internal conditions.
  6. Who is a good candidate for a whole body scan? Individuals with a family history of certain diseases, those experiencing unexplained symptoms, or those seeking a proactive approach to their health may benefit from a whole body scan. However, it’s best to speak with your doctor.
  7. Where can I find a "whole body scan near me"? Use search engines like Google to locate providers in your area. You can also ask your doctor for referrals. Keep in mind that location matters. Always check reviews, too.
  8. What should I expect during a whole body scan? You will typically lie down on a table that slides into the scanner. Depending on the type of scan, you may need to hold your breath at certain times. You will be able to communicate with the technician throughout the procedure.
  9. How do I prepare for a whole body scan? Preparation instructions vary depending on the type of scan. You may be asked to fast for a few hours prior to the scan, and you may need to avoid wearing any metal objects. Your provider will give you specific instructions.
  10. Are alternative options available besides a whole body scan? Yes, depending on your health concerns, your doctor may recommend other imaging tests, such as X-rays, ultrasounds, or specific MRI scans.
  11. How accurate are whole body scans? The accuracy of a whole body scan depends on the technology used and the experience of the radiologist analyzing the images. The best whole-body scans use advanced MRI technology.
  12. Which is the best whole body scanner near me? Evaluate which provider best suits your needs, considering factors like technology used, patient experience, and follow-up care. Check out Advanced Body Scan at https://advancedbodyscan.com/ to learn more about their services.
