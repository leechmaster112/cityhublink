FAQ

What is contractor insurance?

Contractor insurance is a form of business insurance designed to protect contractors from various risks associated with their work, including property damage, bodily injury, and financial losses.

What types of coverage do I need as a contractor?

Common types of coverage include general liability insurance, professional liability insurance, commercial auto insurance, and workers' compensation insurance.

How much does contractor insurance cost?

The cost of contractor insurance varies depending on factors such as the size of your business, the type of work you do, your claims history, and your location.

What is general liability insurance?

General liability insurance protects contractors from claims of bodily injury or property damage caused by their business operations.

What is professional liability insurance (errors & omissions insurance)?

Professional liability insurance protects contractors from claims of financial loss due to errors, omissions, or negligence in their professional services.

Do I need workers' compensation insurance?

If you have employees, workers' compensation insurance is typically required. It covers medical expenses and lost wages for employees injured on the job.

What factors affect contractor insurance rates?

Factors such as the type of work you do, your business's size, your claims history, and your location can all affect your insurance rates.

Is it possible to bundle my contractor insurance policies?

Yes, many insurance providers offer the option to bundle multiple policies (e.g., general liability and commercial auto) for a more cost-effective solution.

How can I reduce my contractor insurance costs?

You can reduce costs by maintaining a good safety record, implementing a strong risk management program, and comparing quotes from multiple insurance companies.

What happens if I don't have contractor insurance?

Without contractor insurance, you could be held personally liable for significant financial losses related to accidents, property damage, or lawsuits, potentially putting your business at risk.
