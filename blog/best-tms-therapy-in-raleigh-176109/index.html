Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

What is TMS therapy and how does it work for depression in Raleigh?

TMS (Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation) therapy is a non-invasive medical procedure that uses magnetic pulses to stimulate nerve cells in the brain. In Raleigh, it's primarily used to treat major depressive disorder, particularly for individuals who haven't responded well to traditional antidepressant medications. The magnetic pulses target specific areas of the brain involved in mood regulation, aiming to improve neuronal activity and alleviate depressive symptoms.

Is TMS therapy considered the best treatment for severe depression in Raleigh?

For severe, treatment-resistant depression, TMS therapy is often considered one of the best and most effective options available in Raleigh. Its non-invasive nature and high success rates for many patients make it a leading alternative when other treatments have failed. Are there any side effects associated with TMS therapy in Raleigh?

TMS therapy is generally well-tolerated. The most common side effect is mild to moderate headache at the treatment site, which usually subsides quickly. Some patients may experience scalp discomfort or facial twitching during stimulation. Serious side effects are rare. Clinicians in Raleigh carefully monitor patients for any adverse reactions.

How long does a typical TMS therapy treatment course take in Raleigh?

A typical TMS therapy course in Raleigh involves daily sessions, usually five days a week, for a period of four to six weeks. Each session typically lasts about 20 to 40 minutes. Your specific treatment plan will be determined by your physician based on your condition and response to treatment.

Can TMS therapy help with conditions other than depression in Raleigh?

While most commonly used for treatment-resistant depression, research and clinical applications for TMS therapy are expanding. In Raleigh, some clinics are exploring its efficacy for conditions like Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder (OCD), anxiety disorders, and post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD). Is TMS therapy a form of electroshock therapy?

No, TMS therapy is distinct from electroconvulsive therapy (ECT), often referred to as electroshock therapy. TMS is non-invasive and uses magnetic pulses, while ECT is a medical procedure where electrical currents are passed through the brain, typically requiring anesthesia and muscle relaxants. TMS generally has fewer side effects and is less disruptive.

After completing a course of TMS therapy in Raleigh, your physician will assess your progress. Many patients experience sustained relief from their symptoms. Some may require maintenance treatments or a combination of TMS with other therapies, such as medication or psychotherapy, to maintain their improvements and prevent relapse. Follow-up care is crucial.
