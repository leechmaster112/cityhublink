Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: What types of epoxy flooring services do you offer at your Pittsburgh location?

We offer a comprehensive range of epoxy flooring services for residential and commercial clients in the Pittsburgh area, including standard clear epoxy, metallic epoxy, solid color epoxy, patterned epoxy (like crackle or marbled effects), and decorative options such as quartz or stone finishes. Q2: How long does an epoxy floor installation typically take at your Pittsburgh business?

The timeline for an epoxy floor project depends on the size of the area, the complexity of the design, and the specific product chosen. Generally, the installation process itself can be completed over a weekend, but the full cure time for the epoxy can take anywhere from 24 to 72 hours before light traffic can resume, and up to 30 days for full cure before heavy use.

Q3: Is epoxy flooring suitable for garages in Pittsburgh?

Absolutely! Epoxy flooring is an excellent choice for garages in Pittsburgh. It provides a durable, skid-resistant surface that can withstand vehicle traffic, spills, and temperature fluctuations. Many homeowners choose epoxy for their garages due to its aesthetic appeal, ease of cleaning, and ability to highlight the concrete surface.

Q4: Do you handle the concrete preparation for epoxy flooring at your Pittsburgh location?

Yes, proper preparation is crucial for a successful epoxy installation. Our team at ArmorX Pittsburgh Epoxy Floors handles all necessary preparation steps at our Pittsburgh location. This includes cleaning the concrete thoroughly, repairing any cracks or damage, and ensuring the surface is ready for optimal adhesion of the epoxy system.

Q5: Can epoxy flooring be applied over existing epoxy in Pittsburgh?

In most cases, yes. Applying a new layer of epoxy over an existing, well-bonded epoxy floor is a common practice. This is often done to refresh the appearance, change colors, or add a new protective topcoat. We would need to inspect the existing surface at our Pittsburgh location to ensure it is in good condition before proceeding with a top-coat application.
