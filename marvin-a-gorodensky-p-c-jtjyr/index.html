Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

Q1: What types of employment law issues do you handle?

A1: Marvin A. Gorodensky P.C focuses exclusively on employment law matters arising in the Toronto area. This includes claims under the Employment Standards Act (ESA), constructive dismissal, wrongful dismissal, human rights complaints related to employment, workplace harassment and discrimination, drafting and reviewing employment contracts, and navigating workplace investigations.

Q2: How long do I have to file a claim related to my employment in Toronto?

A2: Time limits for employment-related claims in Ontario vary depending on the specific issue. For example, ESA complaints generally have a one-year limitation period from the date the violation occurred or was discovered. Wrongful dismissal claims have a three-year limitation period from the date of termination. It is crucial to consult with us promptly to understand the specific time limits applicable to your situation.

Q3: Can you help me if my workplace dispute involves a union?

A3: Yes, we can assist employees and employers in workplace disputes involving unions. While we are not union members or certified collective agreement negotiators, we have experience advising both unionized and non-unionized employees on their rights and entitlements under the relevant collective agreement or the ESA.

Q4: Do I need a lawyer to handle my employment dispute?

A4: While some employment issues can be resolved through negotiation or mediation, complex legal matters often require the expertise of a qualified employment lawyer. A lawyer can provide objective advice, help you understand your legal rights, properly assess the value of your claim, and represent your interests effectively during formal processes like investigations, tribunals, or court proceedings.

Q5: What should I do if I believe I was discriminated against at work?

A5: If you believe you experienced discrimination based on a protected ground, it is advisable to document the incidents in detail, including dates, times, locations, witnesses, and any relevant communications. Contacting us promptly is recommended. We can advise you on the best course of action, which may include attempting to resolve the issue internally or pursuing a formal complaint under the Ontario Human Rights Code.
